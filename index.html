      How to search

      • Japanese — kanji, kana or romaji. Inflected forms work: 食べました, tabemashita.
      • Chinese — traditional or simplified hanzi, or pinyin with or without tones: 你好, ni3hao3, nǐ hǎo, nihao.
      • English — any gloss word or phrase.

      Every query is matched against all three languages at once. The tabs only choose which entries come back: JP ↔ EN gives Japanese entries with English, CN ↔ EN gives Chinese ones, JP ↔ CN gives only entries that have a counterpart on the other side, and All gives everything. Type Chinese with JP ↔ EN selected and you still get the Japanese side, via the pivot. Tab steps through the tabs without leaving the box.

      Hide low scoring drops entries that are archaic, obscure, baby-talk or never attested in the example corpus, unless you typed the word exactly. Turn it off to see everything.

      JP↔CN pairs are derived by matching shared English glosses, so each pairing shows the entry it was matched through. Treat them as suggestions, not authoritative equivalents.
